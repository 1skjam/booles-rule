01 — Overview

What is Boole's Rule?

Boole's Rule (sometimes called Bode's Rule) is a numerical method for approximating the definite integral of a function over a closed interval \([a, b]\). It belongs to the family of Newton–Cotes formulas, which approximate integrals by fitting a polynomial through a set of equally spaced sample points and integrating that polynomial exactly.

Boole's Rule uses five equally spaced points — \(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4\) — and fits a degree-4 polynomial through them. Integrating this polynomial over \([a, b]\) yields a weighted sum of the function values at those five points.

It is more accurate than the Trapezoidal Rule (2 points), Simpson's 1/3 Rule (3 points), and Simpson's 3/8 Rule (4 points), making it a preferred choice when higher precision is needed without resorting to composite methods.

02 — The Formula

Mathematical Formulation

Step Size
\[ h = \frac{b - a}{4} \]

The interval \([a,b]\) is divided into 4 equal subintervals of width \(h\).

Boole's Rule
\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \;\approx\; \frac{2h}{45} \Bigl[7f(x_0) + 32f(x_1) + 12f(x_2) + 32f(x_3) + 7f(x_4)\Bigr] \]

where \(x_i = a + ih\) for \(i = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\).

03 — Weights

Understanding the Weights

The coefficients \(7, 32, 12, 32, 7\) are not arbitrary — they arise from integrating the degree-4 Lagrange interpolating polynomial through the five nodes. Their sum is \(7+32+12+32+7 = 90\), and the leading factor \(\tfrac{2h}{45} = \tfrac{h}{45} \times 2\) ensures the formula is exact for polynomials up to degree 5.

04 — Error Analysis

Error Term & Accuracy

The truncation error for Boole's Rule on a single interval is:

\[ E = -\frac{8}{945} h^7 f^{(6)}(\xi), \quad \xi \in [a, b] \]

The error is of order \(\mathcal{O}(h^7)\), meaning it is exact for all polynomials of degree 5 or less. This gives Boole's Rule significantly higher accuracy than Simpson's Rule (\(\mathcal{O}(h^5)\)).

Method Points Polynomial Degree Error Order
Trapezoidal Rule 2 1 \(\mathcal{O}(h^3)\)
Simpson's 1/3 Rule 3 2 \(\mathcal{O}(h^5)\)
Simpson's 3/8 Rule 4 3 \(\mathcal{O}(h^5)\)
Boole's Rule 5 4 (exact up to 5) \(\mathcal{O}(h^7)\)
